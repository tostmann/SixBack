Who this is for

SixBack keeps every speaker's presets in the ESP32's settings area. That area is small, and each speaker takes a slice of it: roughly 26 entries for a speaker's presets, 28 for the speaker list, 29 for a Spotify login.

With a handful of speakers that is comfortable. At around nine or more it fills up — and once it is full, nothing more can be written, not even the write that would free space again. Symptoms: a preset that stays on PUSH and never corrects itself, a Spotify login that is gone after every restart, settings that silently refuse to save.

SixBack-Max is the ordinary SixBack with a much larger settings area. Everything else is identical — same features, same Web UI, same updates.

Before you flash — two things

1. Your presets will be erased from the stick.
Max rearranges the flash memory, so it can only be installed as a complete fresh write — settings and presets on the stick do not survive it. Export your presets first: in the SixBack Web UI, use the export button on each speaker's preset row and keep the files. After flashing, provide your WiFi again and import them back.
Your speakers themselves keep their presets throughout — it is the stick's copy that is rebuilt.
2. Only for boards with 16 MB flash.
The N16 in a module name means 16 MB. On a smaller board the write fails partway through. If you are unsure which board you have, the board identification page tells you.

Install

Supported: ESP32-S3 and ESP32-C5, both with 16 MB flash. The right image is picked for your chip automatically.

Afterwards

A Max stick reports itself as SixBack-Max — you will see that name in the Web UI status line. Everything else works exactly as before.

Updates work as usual — over WiFi from the stick's own ⚙ SYSTEM → Update, or from this page over USB. A Max stick looks for its updates here under /max/, so it only ever sees releases that actually ship a Max build.

You cannot accidentally mix the two builds: because the names differ, the standard flasher will not update a Max stick, and this page will not update a standard one.

Going back to the standard build works the same way as coming here — a fresh install from the main flasher page, with the same export-first advice.
